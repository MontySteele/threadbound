Sigil vocabulary v2 — shape carries identity

Three orthogonal axes, each readable at a glance. Archetype — the silhouette family — says what kind of thing it is. Tier dress says what rank it holds. Act sets accent hue and frame-tick style. Constant throughlines hold the set together: the crossing thread, the grave-mark brand, the pale line language, the same ground. Glow is cut back; shape does the talking.

·all marks deterministic from id + motif map

Axis 1 — archetypes (silhouette families)

Every entity is assigned one family in a small motif map (data, not code). Families are built from the same primitives — seeded closed paths, knots, rim light — so they read as one hand.

Axis 2 — tier dress (same entity, five ranks)

Rank never changes the silhouette — it changes the framing. Minions shrink and lose their corner ticks. Elites tatter: notched double rim, barbed frame. Bosses take the broken crown, the inscription ring, a shoulder mass, a doubled frame. Player characters are the only marks held in an unbroken ring — bound and whole, where every enemy ring is broken.

Axis 3 — act (same entity, three descents)

Accent pool shifts per act, and the frame ticks change construction: Act 1 squared (lamplit earth), Act 2 curved (the Choir), Act 3 frayed (the Braid coming apart).
